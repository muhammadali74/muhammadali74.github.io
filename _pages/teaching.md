During my undergrad, I worked as a Teaching Assistant for a few different courses. It was a great experience. I got to help students, explain concepts, and learn a lot myself in the process.

Over time, I noticed a lot of students were struggling with the same kinds of things. So, based on the questions I got (and from my own undergrad experience), here’s some honest advice that might help if you’re taking similar courses:

### Start things early (seriously)
It’s so tempting to wait until the night before a deadline, but trust me—starting even a little bit early makes everything way less stressful. Plus, you’ll actually have time to ask for help if you get stuck.

### Ask questions—even the “dumb” ones
If something doesn’t make sense, speak up. Whether it's in class, office hours, or even online, chances are someone else is confused too. No one’s judging you.

### Give it some time (Let it sink in)
There will be times when you come across a concept that just doesn’t click right away. This is especially true in courses that aren’t exactly your strong suit. And that’s completely normal.
When that happens, don’t assume you're not smart enough to get it. Some topics just need a bit more time to sink in. Step away from it, revisit it later, and go over it a few times from different angles—watch a video, read an explanation, try solving a problem related to it. Slowly but surely, things start to connect.

### Practice > Reading
Just reading the textbook or watching lectures won’t cut it. Try the problems, write the code, do the exercises. Struggling through it is part of the process—that’s where the real learning happens.

### Find the right study group
Studying with others can be super helpful, but make sure it’s a group where people actually want to understand the material—not just copy answers.

### Go to office hours (and come prepared)
Office hours are gold. Show up with specific questions or things you don’t get. You’ll get way more out of it than just showing up blank.

### Try explaining things to others
If you can explain a concept to someone else without looking at your notes, you probably really get it. Teaching is one of the best ways to learn.
