Research Internship at Texas A&M University

Created on July 30, 2024

2024

Completed my 3-month research internship at Texas A&M University!

This summer, I had the incredible opportunity to join the Mixed Signals Lab at the Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering, Texas A&M University, under the supervision of Dr. Sebastian Hoyos. TAMU—one of the largest and most respected public research universities in the United States—is renowned for its strong engineering tradition, advanced research facilities, and a collaborative academic culture.
The Zachry Engineering Education Complex, in particular, stood out as a remarkable space—open, modern, and designed to spark innovation through hands-on exploration and interdisciplinary thinking.

Zachary Engineering Complex (left). Me at the J.Earl Rudder tower (right)

During the internship, I worked alongside my colleague Muslim Hussain, a student from TAMU Fadwa, and PhD student Haotian Zhao. Our research focused on noise reduction in the spectral calibration of Swept-Source Optical Coherence Tomography (SS-OCT) systems—a crucial step toward improving imaging accuracy in biomedical applications.

We began by reproducing existing signal-denoising methods based on Kalman filtering, and then explored how deep learning could further enhance noise reduction.
I modeled the calibration-signal denoising task using LSTM networks, trained the models on representative OCT data, and evaluated their effectiveness compared to classical filtering approaches. It was exciting to see the model consistently reduce residual noise and smooth the calibration signal more effectively than existing methods.

At the end of the internship, we presented our work in a research poster session. It was encouraging to receive positive feedback from faculty, researchers, and students who showed great interest in the machine-learning component of our work.

This experience strengthened my interest in the intersection of machine learning, signal processing, and biomedical systems, and gave me the chance to work in an environment that thrives on research excellence, curiosity, and collaboration. I’m grateful to Dr. Hoyos and the Mixed Signals Lab for their mentorship and guidance throughout these months.

Excited for what’s next!
