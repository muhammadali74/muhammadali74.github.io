We Won the **FBL Fintech Hackathon 2024**.
--

In Fall 2024, I, along with my batchmates **Ali Nisar**, **Neha**, and **Maaz**, took part in the Fintech Hackathon organized by **Faysal Bank**. The challenge required participants to design meaningful solutions for the future of banking in Pakistan.  
Our goal was to create a solution that addresses the banking needs of visually impaired individuals. 

We approached this challenge using the **design thinking methodology** taught at Habib University, inspired by the Stanford d.school framework. We conducted interviews, carried out secondary research, explored banking accessibility guidelines, and mapped out the specific pain points visually impaired users face when interacting with modern banking apps.

During the initial phase, we tested the existing Faysal Bank mobile app using Google’s Accessibility Scanner. On a single screen, the tool reported **22 accessibility problems**, ranging from missing labels to poor contrast and non-descriptive UI elements. It became clear that a standard visual interface is not compatible with the needs of someone who cannot see the screen in the first place.

To address this, we began ideating solutions that do not rely on traditional UI at all.  
We explored:

- **Haptic feedback** as a primary navigation tool  
- **Audio-based guidance** for every action  
- Simplified, sequential interactions instead of dense screens -- something which may look ugly to look at but eases app usage for these individuals.  
- Compliance with **State Bank of Pakistan’s digital banking guidelines**  
- Security practices adapted for non-visual flows  

After weeks of prototyping, testing with accessibility tools, and refining the interaction model, we developed **Faysal Bank Envision** — a redesigned banking experience that can be *heard* and *felt*.  
Our prototypes reflected a complete user journey, including login, balance check, payments, transfers, and navigation between sections, all made accessible without relying on visual cues.

We ensured that each iteration passed Google’s accessibility checks and aligned with the constraints of regulated banking applications. Throughout the process, we focused on building something feasible, compliant, and genuinely usable.

At the end of the hackathon, we presented our final prototype to the jury. The solution was well received, and our team was declared the winner of the competition. We later presented the project again to **Mr. Yousaf Hussain**, President & CEO of Faysal Bank.

This project allowed us to work on a problem rooted in empathy and user-centered design. It challenged us to think beyond the visual interface and consider what it means to build technology for people whose interaction with the world is shaped differently.  
It was a valuable experience in structured problem-solving, accessibility design, and rapid prototyping.
